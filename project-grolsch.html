UX research · B2B web usability

Grolsch Connect

Eye-tracking and usability testing exposed ordering friction in a wholesale portal.

My role

Lead UX Researcher & Usability Analyst

Context & scope

University of Twente BMS Lab usability project for Royal Grolsch N.V.; 9 participants.

Methods & tools

Tobii Pro Fusion eye-tracking, think-aloud testing, System Usability Scale

Grolsch Connect Interface

The Brief

Evaluate the tank-beer ordering journey in Grolsch Connect and identify ways to support hospitality customers moving from phone orders to self-service.

The Problem

Participants had trouble finding previous orders and some missed the minimum order quantity, which was presented in a hover tooltip. Important transaction information was hidden behind interaction patterns that weren't discoverable.

Key Insights

Important transaction information should be visible without hover, and frequently used ordering tasks should not be buried in account navigation. Eye-tracking data revealed that users didn't explore interactive elements they couldn't immediately see.

Design Recommendations

Proposed a simpler ordering path, inline minimum-order information, clearer price and delivery details, and more direct access to saved orders. These changes would reduce cognitive load and support the transition to self-service purchasing.

Testing Results

6 out of 9 participants missed the minimum order quantity indicator. 5 out of 9 encountered navigation errors when trying to place repeat orders. The System Usability Scale indicated moderate usability issues that required structural changes rather than minor refinements.

Outcome

The study produced a focused redesign framework prioritizing information visibility, reducing decision friction, and streamlining the ordering workflow for B2B customers. These findings inform the next iteration of Grolsch Connect's user experience.
